# The Cypress Throne

## Setting
Historical fantasy in the sea-ringed empire of Orsanna, centered on the hill capital of Selucor during nine days of mourning after an emperor's death. A boy prince is due to be crowned while a peace embassy arrives from the steppe kingdom of Kardesh. You return from exile as a physician-scribe because you can read the old river script used in the lost Covenant of Bridges, a founding pact that may decide whether the realm chooses peace, war, or something older than either.

## Core Tone
- Lyrical but restrained
- Courtly, political, and intimate
- Focused on memory, duty, empire, and the cost of choices
- Supernatural elements should stay subtle: dreams, shrine signs, uncanny echoes, and old places that remember

## Main Characters
- You: exiled physician-scribe called back to court
- Regent Iria Valen: the dead emperor's sister, holding the empire together
- Captain Soren Vale: frontier cavalry commander and an old trusted bond from your past
- Envoy Tamar Kesri: the sharp, composed Kardeshi envoy seeking peace on hard terms
- Mother Calia: keeper of the cypress shrine beneath the palace hill
- Prince Alexen: the young heir, observant and more serious than the court realizes
- Chancellor Marek Danos: polished court operator with a talent for profitable chaos
- Lady Danira Sol: harbor merchant whose loyalties depend on whether peace seems real

## Key Story Points
1. Return to Selucor and re-enter a court shaped by mourning, memory, and suspicion
2. Learn that part of the Covenant of Bridges is missing or hidden
3. Navigate palace, harbor, shrine, archive, and frontier networks without letting the branches sprawl
4. Use natural redirect events to separate and reconnect story threads
5. Discover who benefits from continued raids and broken peace
6. Decide how truth should be used at the coronation and treaty ceremony

## Main Branches
1. Palace branch
   - Regent, prince, council, and chancellor politics
2. Harbor branch
   - Merchants, grain, embassy logistics, and crowd unrest
3. Shrine branch
   - Old cypress rites, dream signs, and hidden memory
4. Frontier branch
   - Soren, riders, refugees, raids, and military pressure
5. Archive branch
   - Tablets, seals, dispatches, maps, and the covenant text

## Redirect Design
- Use more redirect nodes than usual
- Make the randomness feel historical and physical, not arbitrary
- Good redirect causes: crowd surges, processions, river fog, storms, messenger errors, bolting horses, feast seating, shrine bells, courtyard panic, and market tumult
- Several redirect outcomes should backtrack naturally to earlier hubs such as the archive, palace prep, ferry route, or previous decision centers

## Possible Endings
1. Peace of Bridges: the covenant is restored and a durable peace is publicly chosen
2. Hidden Chronicle: the prince rules through truth and memory rather than spectacle
3. Iron Falcon: war wins the court, securing the realm at a moral cost
4. Cypress Oath: the old pact is renewed through shrine and ceremony, changing the shape of power
5. Road of Dust: you leave court with the truth preserved, refusing to become one more ornament of empire

## Writing Notes
- Keep paragraphs short and vivid
- Keep node labels short and specific to the scene
- Make action labels clearly match the message content
- Aim for roughly 140 nodes with 5 valid finish nodes
- Keep branches intertwined and frequently merged
- Make backtracking feel like delay, uncertainty, or changing circumstance rather than punishment
