# Moonlit Bellarose

## Setting
Romantic drama in the cliffside festival town of Bellarose during the three-night Moon Lantern Festival. The player returns to help Aunt Mireille reopen the old glass conservatory just as Julien Marceau, a violinist and almost-love from one unforgettable summer, comes home with a possible Paris offer hanging over him.

## Core Tone
- Tender, moonlit, emotionally honest, and slightly playful
- Romance driven by timing, missed meetings, and repeated second chances
- Chance events should feel natural: weather, crowds, misdelivered notes, parade detours, drifting lanterns, and festival noise

## Main Characters
- You: returning to Bellarose to help restore the conservatory
- Julien Marceau: violinist, warm but evasive when afraid of wanting too much
- Aunt Mireille: owner of the conservatory, practical and quietly romantic
- Celia Hart: friend who runs the lantern boats and gives grounded advice
- Theo Laurent: pastry chef friend, teasing but loyal
- Inez Vale: maskmaker and festival stylist
- Madame Sabine D'Orsay: elegant patron pushing Julien toward Paris

## Key Story Points
1. Re-enter Bellarose and reunite with Julien in the middle of festival preparations
2. Rebuild familiarity through the conservatory, market, pier, ballroom, and cliff gardens
3. Use letters, masks, and music as repeating romantic motifs
4. Let random festival events separate and reconnect the pair several times
5. Bring in the Paris rumor and Sabine's pressure without turning the story cruel
6. Converge everything at the final lantern night and force a clear emotional choice

## Story Structure
1. Arrival and first reunion
   - First glimpse of Julien from different entry points
   - Initial tone set by warmth, teasing, or guarded distance
2. Afternoon threads
   - Conservatory work
   - Promenade walk
   - Market errands
   - Early personal questions about the past and Paris
3. Chance-driven middle
   - Redirect nodes for crowd surges, sudden rain, scattered envelopes, parade reroutes, fireworks, and misty delays
   - Several redirect outcomes should naturally send the player back to an earlier hub instead of only forward
4. Emotional pressure
   - Secret notes
   - Missed meetings
   - Sabine's arrival and the Paris choice
   - Advice scenes with Celia, Theo, and Mireille
5. Final convergence
   - Last lantern night
   - A bridge meeting, a train choice, or a decision to stay rooted in Bellarose

## Branch Design
- Keep 4-5 main active branches at most, with frequent merges
- Aim for roughly 120 nodes
- Use more redirect nodes than usual, especially in the middle acts
- Redirects should sometimes return the player to promenade, conservatory, or planning hubs in a way that feels like festival chaos rather than arbitrary looping
- Action labels must stay tightly matched to the scene text and stay short

## Recurring Motifs
- Moonflowers opening at night
- Silver masks
- Folded letters with pressed petals
- Violin music drifting through crowded streets
- Lantern boats and cliffside wind

## Possible Endings
1. Bellarose Kiss: you and Julien choose each other openly and stay in Bellarose together
2. Paris Waltz: you leave together for Paris, turning uncertainty into adventure
3. Steady Bloom: you choose your own rooted life in Bellarose, with romance left tender but unresolved
4. Season of Letters: you choose a slower, long-distance courtship built on honest promises
5. Missed Tide: the moment passes softly, leaving a bittersweet memory instead of bitterness

## Writing Notes
- Keep every node to short paragraphs, usually 2-3 sentences per text line at most
- Use at least one emoji per node
- Keep the romance sincere, not melodramatic
- Make the backtracking feel like missed timing, not punishment
